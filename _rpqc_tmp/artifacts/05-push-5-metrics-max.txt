Design notes for the team
One message, one section. “Stop at $1.5M” and “what if you maxed out” live in one navy section, joined by “But… what if you did?”.
Bigger headline, and “Stop here · your goal” / “Or go all the way” are now small eyebrows, not badges.
Card sizes tell the story: the $1.5M card is deliberately smaller; the max card is the biggest thing on screen, white with a green outline, so both cards stand right off the dark background.
Where they are → where they could go: today, goal and max are drawn as bars on one scale, with the growth multiple on each (6.6× and 12×).
Scepticism block: straight after the bars, we admit 12× is a stretch and show the path as steps: fix one metric at a time, 3× → 5× → 8× → 12×. The middle steps are illustrative, not promised. Jimmy: pull 2–3 client results of 12× or more from the homepage into the proof cards.
New quiz logic, 12× cap: the “all 5 metrics at max” number can never be more than 12× today. Max RPV = the lower of (RPV with all five metrics at their ceilings) and (12 × current RPV). Max added revenue = (max RPV − current RPV) × annual visitors. In this example: 12 × $0.75 = $9.00 RPV, so ~$3.24M/year in total and +$2.97M on top of today, instead of the uncapped $10.6M.
Edge case to decide: for very small businesses the $1.5M goal itself can be more than 12× today (e.g. $60K/year today needs 26× to add $1.5M). Should the cap win there, or the goal?
For the build: “today” must use each person’s real current revenue, not the worked-back estimate in this mock-up. The ~$270K figure happens to match the $10k–35k/month revenue tier ($270,000), but please confirm the formula with whoever owns the calculator.
